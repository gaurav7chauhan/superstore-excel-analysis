# Superstore Sales Analysis in Excel

I used the Superstore sales dataset to find out which regions, categories and products actually make money, and which ones quietly lose it.

## About the Data
The data comes from Kaggle. It has 9,994 orders across 21 columns, covering sales, profit, discounts, customers and regions.

## What I Wanted to Find Out
- Which regions and categories make the most (and least) profit
- Which products sell well but still lose money
- How much discounts really affect profit

## How I Did It
1. **Cleaned the data:** fixed the date formats and turned the data into an Excel Table.
2. **Analyzed it:** built PivotTables to break down sales and profit by region, category, sub-category, customer, segment and ship mode.
3. **Built a dashboard:** put the key charts together so the results are easy to read at a glance.

## What I Found

**Regions**
- West is my strongest region and made the most profit.
- Central made the least profit, so it needs the most attention.

**Products**
- Bookcases, Tables and Supplies lose money even though they sell. Their prices and discounts are worth a closer look.

**Discounts**
- This was the clearest pattern. Orders with no discount made about $66 profit on average, while heavily discounted orders lost about $106 on average. Big discounts clearly hurt profit.

**Growth over time**
- Sales dipped about 2.8% in 2015, then grew about 29% in 2016 and 20% in 2017.

**Customers and shipping**
- Tamara Chand is the most profitable customer, with $8,981 in profit.
- Consumer is the most profitable segment, with $134,119 in profit.
- Standard Class is the most profitable ship mode, with $164,088 in profit.

## What I'd Recommend
- Cut back on heavy discounts, since they turn profitable sales into losses.
- Review the pricing and discounts on Bookcases, Tables and Supplies.
- Look into why Central is underperforming and what the West is doing right.
- Keep looking after high-value customers like Tamara Chand.
